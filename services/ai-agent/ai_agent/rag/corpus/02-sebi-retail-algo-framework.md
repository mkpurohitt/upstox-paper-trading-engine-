# SEBI Framework for Retail Algorithmic Trading

## Overview

In February 2025 SEBI issued a circular on "Safer participation of retail investors in Algorithmic trading". It allows retail investors to use algorithms through their stock brokers' APIs, while making brokers accountable as principals for every algo that trades through them. Implementation was phased in by the exchanges during late 2025; always check the latest NSE/BSE circulars for current dates and formats, because operational details (registration forms, order-rate thresholds, tagging formats) have been refined over time.

## Key requirements

**Brokers are principals.** Brokers are responsible for algos used by their clients, must handle investor grievances related to them, and must obtain exchange approval for each algo strategy offered.

**Algo registration and unique Algo-ID.** Algorithms must be registered with the exchange through the broker. Each approved algo receives a unique identifier, and every order generated by that algo must carry the Algo-ID so that the exchange can trace orders to strategies. Until an exchange-assigned ID exists, AlgoDesk stores a placeholder (ALGO-PENDING-S{strategy}) on every order and deployment so that the field can be populated after empanelment.

**Order-rate threshold.** Retail clients who use APIs to place orders above a specified order-per-second threshold (10 orders per second per exchange per client, as set by the exchanges) must register their algo even if it is self-built. Below that threshold, orders from "tech-savvy" retail users can be treated as non-algo orders, but they must still be placed through the broker's authenticated API. AlgoDesk enforces a configurable per-second ceiling (default 8 per second) below the threshold, plus per-minute limits.

**White-box vs black-box algos.** White-box (execution) algos have logic that is disclosed and replicable. Black-box algos, whose logic is not disclosed, may only be offered by providers registered with SEBI as Research Analysts, who must maintain a research report for each algo and seek fresh registration whenever the logic changes.

**Algo providers and empanelment.** Third-party algo providers (fintechs, vendors) must be empanelled with exchanges through brokers. They are agents of the broker, not independently regulated intermediaries (unless registered as RAs for black-box offerings).

**API access controls.** Open APIs are not permitted. Brokers must provide API access only through a unique vendor-client specific API key and a static IP whitelisted by the broker, with OAuth-based authentication and two-factor authentication. API sessions must log out before the next trading session begins.

**Static IP.** Clients and algo providers must route API orders through a registered static IP address. A family can share one static IP. AlgoDesk documents the egress IP to whitelist (EGRESS_STATIC_IP) and routes broker traffic through it in production.

**Kill switch and risk controls.** Brokers must be able to halt any algo instantly (a "kill switch") and apply pre-trade risk checks. AlgoDesk exposes a user kill switch, an admin platform-wide kill switch and pre-trade limits (quantity, lots, position value, orders per minute, daily loss) that apply to every order.

**Audit trail.** Orders, modifications and cancellations generated by algos must be logged and retained. AlgoDesk keeps an append-only, hash-chained audit log that cannot be updated or deleted through the database.

## What this means for users

- Strategies must be deployed through a broker you have connected with valid API credentials and 2FA.
- Live algo deployments require the platform's risk checks to pass and are tagged with strategy and algo identifiers.
- If you plan to exceed the order-rate threshold you need exchange registration through your broker.
- Anyone selling algorithmic strategies with undisclosed logic to the public needs SEBI Research Analyst registration.
- Nothing in the framework guarantees profits; algos can lose money quickly, especially in derivatives. SEBI's own studies of FY22–FY24 found that a large majority (about 9 in 10) of individual F&O traders made net losses.

## Investment advice

Giving buy/sell/hold recommendations on specific securities for consideration requires registration as an Investment Adviser (IA) or Research Analyst (RA). Platforms without such registration must limit themselves to education, data and tools. AlgoDesk's copilot therefore runs with ADVICE_ENABLED=false by default.
